Eligibility Criteria, Academic Diversity & Work Experience

Detailed breakdown of qualifying academic credentials, final-year undergraduate rules, government reservations, and how academic and gender diversity points influence IIM calls.

🎓

Qualifying Degree Requirements

Candidates must hold a Bachelor's Degree with a minimum of 50% marks or equivalent CGPA (relaxable to 45% for SC, ST, and PwD candidates) awarded by any recognized university or institution established by an Act of Parliament.

⏳

Final Year Students Eligible

Students appearing for the final year of their Bachelor's degree (or equivalent qualification) and those awaiting final semester results are fully eligible to apply. Provisional admission requires completing all exams before the specified joining deadline.

📜

Professional Qualifications

Candidates who have successfully passed professional qualifying certifications such as CA (Chartered Accountancy), CS (Company Secretary), or ICWA/CMA with the requisite aggregate percentage are fully eligible to appear.

⚖️

No Age Limit or Attempt Cap

There is no upper or lower age restriction to appear for CAT 2027. Aspirants can attempt the examination an unlimited number of times, making it accessible to fresh graduates as well as experienced working professionals.

🌐

Reservation Mandates

Central government reservation quotas apply: 15% for SC, 7.5% for ST, 27% for NC-OBC, 10% for General-EWS, and 5% horizontal reservation for Persons with Benchmark Disabilities (PwD).

🌟

Academic & Gender Diversity

To cultivate balanced cohorts, premier IIMs award 2 to 5 bonus composite points for non-engineering disciplines (Arts, Commerce, Medicine, Law) and 2 to 5 bonus points for female and transgender candidates.

⚠️ Crucial Rule: Strict Sectional Lock
In CAT 2027, you cannot switch back and forth between sections. You must complete Section I (VARC) during the first 40 minutes. The moment the 40-minute timer expires, Section I automatically locks and submits, transitioning you to Section II (DILR), followed similarly by Section III (QA). PwD candidates are allotted an additional 13 minutes and 20 seconds per section (53 minutes and 20 seconds per section; 160 minutes total).

In-Depth Sectional Anatomy & Question Formats

Examine the structural design, typical passage lengths, set patterns, and mathematical focus areas across each of the three CAT test sections.

📖

Section I: VARC (24 Questions)

Reading Comprehension (16 Questions): Consists of 4 passages (~400 to 500 words each) carrying 4 questions per passage. Genres include Philosophy, Sociology, Economics, Psychology, Science & Technology, Art, and History. Questions test inference, main idea, author attitude, and tone.

Verbal Ability (8 Questions): Parajumbles (4 sentences to reorder), Paragraph Summary (identifying the optimal summary), Odd Sentence Out, and Sentence Placement (inserting a given sentence into the logical paragraph slot).

📊

Section II: DILR (20 Questions)

Set Structure: Typically comprises 4 comprehensive problem sets containing 5 questions each (or a combination of 5-question and 6-question sets).

Core Domains: Reasoning-based Data Interpretation (missing tables, spider radar charts), Games & Tournaments (round-robin, knockout schedules), Matrix & Grid Arrangements, Routes & Networks, Binary Logic (truth-tellers and liars), and Set Theory Venn Diagrams with 4 variables.

📐

Section III: QA (22 Questions)

Arithmetic (~8 to 10 Questions): The highest weightage area: Percentages, Profit & Loss, Simple/Compound Interest, Ratio & Proportion, Time & Work, Time-Speed-Distance, Averages, and Mixtures & Alligations.

Algebra & Geometry (~8 to 10 Questions): Linear & Quadratic Equations, Logarithms, Sequences & Series (AP/GP), Functions, Inequalities, Modulus, Triangles, Circles, Polygons, Coordinate Geometry, and Mensuration. Modern Math (P&C, Probability) contributes 2–3 questions.

CAP Cutoff Notification

Following CAT results in January, the coordinating IIM (rotational, e.g., IIM Kashipur or IIM Udaipur) releases the unified minimum qualifying sectional and overall percentiles required for CAP registration.

Centralized CAP Registration

Eligible candidates log in to the official IIM CAP portal, upload verified graduation certificates, 10th/12th marksheets, work experience proofs, and category certificates before the late-January deadline.

Stage 3

Single Unified Personal Interview

Shortlisted applicants attend ONE unified Personal Interview (online/offline). Panels comprise experienced senior faculty members from multiple participating IIMs who evaluate the candidate holistically.

Stage 4

Independent Merit List Compilation

The interview score is transmitted to all participating IIMs. Each individual IIM applies its distinct composite formula (weighting CAT score, PI score, work ex, and diversity) to generate its final admission offers.

Comprehensive Syllabus & 8-Month Preparation Strategy

Tested month-by-month study architecture, section-wise mastery tactics, recommended literature, and mock analysis methods to breach the 99th percentile mark.

🎯

Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1–3)

Concept Mastery: Complete all core Arithmetic concepts (Percentages, Ratios, Time-Work, Speed-Distance) and basic Algebra. Read 2 diverse editorial articles daily (The Hindu, Aeon Essays, Project Syndicate). Solve 2 DILR sets daily from past question papers without timers.

⚡

Phase 2: Practice & Sectionals (Months 4–6)

Timed Drills: Begin 40-minute sectional tests. Master RC speed reading (aim for 250–300 words/min with high comprehension). Identify your 'comfort' DILR themes (Arrangements vs. DI vs. Tournaments). Cover Modern Math, Geometry, and Number Systems.

🏆

Phase 3: Mocks & Strategy (Months 7–8)

Full-Length Simulator: Attempt 25 to 35 full-length Computer Based Mock Tests under strict exam-slot conditions (Slot 1, 2, or 3). Dedicate 4 hours to in-depth analysis per mock: review every unattempted question, analyze negative marking causes, and optimize question-skipping discipline.

Frequently Asked Questions: CAT 2027

Clear, authoritative answers to the most common queries regarding CAT registration, scoring formulas, normalization, reservations, and IIM interview selection.

The Common Admission Test (CAT 2027) is India's most prestigious computer-based management entrance examination conducted annually on a rotational basis by the Indian Institutes of Management (IIMs). It serves as the primary admission benchmark for postgraduate management programs (MBA, PGP, PGDM, Executive MBA, and Ph.D.) across all 21 IIMs (including IIM Ahmedabad, Bangalore, Calcutta, Lucknow, Kozhikode, Indore, Mumbai, and 10+ CAP IIMs) as well as over 1,200 premier non-IIM business schools including FMS Delhi, SPJIMR Mumbai, MDI Gurgaon, IIT Bombay (SJMSOM), IIT Delhi (DMS), IIT Kharagpur (VGSoM), IIT Madras (DoMS), JBIMS Mumbai, and BITSoM.
Yes, absolutely. Candidates appearing for the final year of their Bachelor's degree (or equivalent qualification) and those who have completed degree requirements and are awaiting results are fully eligible to apply. If selected, such candidates are admitted provisionally, provided they submit a certificate from the Principal/Registrar of their university certifying that all final examinations have been completed before the specified cutoff date (usually June 30 of the admission year).
Candidates must hold a Bachelor's Degree in any discipline with at least 50% marks or equivalent CGPA for General, General-EWS, and NC-OBC categories. For candidates belonging to Scheduled Caste (SC), Scheduled Tribe (ST), and Persons with Benchmark Disabilities (PwD), the minimum qualifying requirement is 45% marks or equivalent CGPA. Candidates with professional qualifications like CA, CS, ICWA/CMA, or FCAI with the requisite percentage are also eligible.
CAT 2027 is a 2-hour (120 minutes) Computer-Based Test (CBT) consisting of 66 questions totaling 198 marks. The examination is divided into three sections with a mandatory 40-minute sectional lock: Section I: Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension (VARC) - 24 Questions (72 marks); Section II: Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning (DILR) - 20 Questions (60 marks); Section III: Quantitative Aptitude (QA) - 22 Questions (66 marks). Candidates cannot switch between sections freely; each section must be completed within its designated 40-minute window.
For Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs), candidates are awarded +3 marks for every correct answer and penalized -1 mark for every incorrect answer. For Non-MCQ or TITA (Type In The Answer) questions, where candidates enter numerical or text values via an on-screen virtual keypad, candidates receive +3 marks for a correct response, and there is ZERO negative marking (no penalty for incorrect answers). Unattempted questions carry zero marks.
Because CAT is administered in three distinct shifts across morning, afternoon, and evening sessions, a rigorous multi-session normalization procedure is applied. First, candidate raw scores are calculated. Second, raw scores are adjusted through mathematical percentile normalization to derive Scaled Scores across each section and overall, adjusting for subtle variances in shift difficulty. Third, Scaled Scores are converted into All India Percentiles, representing the percentage of candidates who scored equal to or less than that candidate.
Historically, achieving a 99+ percentile in CAT out of a total 198 marks requires a net raw score of approximately 72 to 78 marks (roughly 36% to 40% of the total paper). For a 95 percentile, a raw score of 52 to 58 marks is typically sufficient. For a 90 percentile, approximately 43 to 48 marks are required. Balancing sectional cutoffs is equally vital, as top IIMs require 75 to 85+ percentile in every individual section (VARC, DILR, and QA).
IIM CAP (Common Admission Process) is a centralized, single-window selection procedure for Written Ability Test (WAT) and Personal Interview (PI) conducted for 10+ new and baby IIMs (such as IIM Udaipur, IIM Trichy, IIM Ranchi, IIM Raipur, IIM Kashipur, IIM Jammu, IIM Bodh Gaya, IIM Sambalpur, and IIM Sirmaur). Instead of appearing for separate interviews at each campus, shortlisted candidates attend one unified CAP interview, and individual participating IIMs generate their own merit lists using their internal composite scoring formulas.
IIM admissions do not rely solely on CAT percentiles. Top institutes calculate a Composite Score out of 100 which typically distributes weightage as: CAT Score (35% to 55%), Class 10 Board Marks (10% to 15%), Class 12 Board Marks (10% to 15%), Graduation Marks (10% to 15%), Quality & Duration of Work Experience (5% to 10%, peaking at 24-36 months), Academic Diversity (2% to 5% for non-engineering disciplines), and Gender Diversity (2% to 5% for female/transgender candidates). Shortlisted candidates then undergo Personal Interview (PI) and Written Ability Test (WAT) which carry 30% to 50% weight in the final selection stage.

However, for non-IIM institutions that accept CAT scores (such as FMS Delhi, SPJIMR Mumbai, MDI Gurgaon, IIT Bombay SJMSOM, IIT Delhi DMS, JBIMS, BITSoM, etc.), candidates MUST apply separately on each respective institute portal before their individual deadlines, entering their CAT Registration Number.
