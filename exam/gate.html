GATE 2027 Examination & Admission Overview

Administered on behalf of the National Coordination Board (NCB) - GATE, Department of Higher Education, Ministry of Education, Government of India, the Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering is India's most prestigious benchmark for postgraduate engineering education and executive public sector employment.

Full Examination Name Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering (GATE 2027)
Conducting Authorities Jointly administered by IISc Bangalore and 7 IITs (IIT Bombay, IIT Delhi, IIT Guwahati, IIT Kanpur, IIT Kharagpur, IIT Madras, IIT Roorkee) on a rotational basis
Mode of Examination 100% Computer-Based Test (CBT) across designated TCS iON digital testing centers nationwide and international centers
Duration & Sessions 3 Hours (180 Minutes) · 2 Sessions Daily (Forenoon: 9:30 AM – 12:30 PM, Afternoon: 2:30 PM – 5:30 PM) across two consecutive weekends in February
Total Test Papers 30 Individual Test Papers (Disciplines) with allowed Two-Paper Combinations
Total Questions & Marks 65 Questions = 100 Total Marks (General Aptitude: 15 Marks + Subject Specific Core & Mathematics: 85 Marks)
Question Types Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ), Multiple Select Questions (MSQ), and Numerical Answer Type (NAT)
Marking & Negative Marking 1-mark MCQs: -1/3 penalty · 2-mark MCQs: -2/3 penalty · MSQs & NATs carry ZERO negative marking · No partial marks in MSQs
Score Scale & Validity Raw Marks out of 100 scaled to a GATE Normalized Score out of 1000 · Valid for 3 years for academic admissions · Valid for current year for PSU recruitment
Key Portals GOAPS (Application & Admit Card) · COAP (IIT M.Tech Offers) · CCMT (NIT/IIIT Admissions)
Monthly Scholarship ₹12,400 per month MoE Financial Assistance for all GATE-qualified students admitted to M.Tech/M.S. in MoE-funded institutes

Direct gateway to M.Tech, M.E., M.S. (Research), and Direct Ph.D. programs at IISc Bangalore, 23 IITs, 31 NITs, IIITs, IIEST Shibpur, and centrally-funded technical institutions with monthly stipend support.

GATE 2027 Exam Pattern, Section Distribution & Marking Scheme

Master the structure of the 3-hour Computer Based Test (CBT). Understand how MCQs, MSQs, and NATs are evaluated, and learn how negative marking works.

⏱️ 180 Minutes (3 Hours)

Total of 65 questions carrying 100 marks. The clock is displayed continuously on the top right corner of the CBT console.

🧠 General Aptitude (GA)

Mandatory for all 30 test papers. 10 questions worth 15 marks (5 questions of 1-mark + 5 questions of 2-marks) testing verbal, numerical, and spatial aptitude.

📐 Subject Core & Maths

55 questions worth 85 marks covering Engineering Mathematics (~13-15 marks where applicable) and Core Discipline syllabus (~70-72 marks).

Question Type Format & Answering Method Marks per Question Negative Marking Penalty Partial Marking Policy
Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ) Objective type with 4 options; strictly 1 correct option. Selected using mouse click. 1 Mark or 2 Marks 1-mark: -1/3 Mark
2-mark: -2/3 Mark 		No partial marking. Zero marks for unattempted questions.
Multiple Select Questions (MSQ) Objective type with 4 choices; one or more choices can be correct (1, 2, 3, or all 4). 1 Mark or 2 Marks ZERO Negative Marking (0) No partial credit. You must choose ALL correct choices and NO incorrect choice to get full marks.
Numerical Answer Type (NAT) Numeric input question. Value must be entered using the on-screen virtual numeric keypad with mouse. 1 Mark or 2 Marks ZERO Negative Marking (0) No negative marks. Acceptable answers lie within a strict numerical range defined by the test creators.
⚠️ Virtual Scientific Calculator Notice
Candidates are strictly forbidden from bringing physical calculators into the examination center. An on-screen Virtual Scientific Calculator is integrated directly into the CBT interface. Familiarize yourself with memory functions, trigonometric inverses, exponential operators, and parentheses on the official mock simulator.

COAP & CCMT Dual Counselling Roadmap Explained

Postgraduate engineering admissions in India operate through two distinct centralized portals. Understanding how COAP (for IITs) differs from CCMT (for NITs & IIITs) is crucial to avoid losing allotted seats.

🏛️ Pathway 1: COAP (for IISc & 23 IITs)

Common Offer Acceptance Portal (COAP) is NOT an application portal. Candidates must apply individually to each IIT's postgraduate portal, and register on COAP to view and accept offers.

  • Accept & Freeze: You accept the offer, lock the seat, and exit further COAP rounds.
  • Retain & Wait: You hold the current offer and participate in subsequent rounds for higher preferences. Maximum 2 Retain & Wait on the same offer.
  • Reject & Wait: You reject all current offers and wait for higher rounds.
  • 5 Main Rounds followed by Additional / Spot Rounds.

🏢 Pathway 2: CCMT (for 31 NITs & IIITs)

Centralized Counselling for M.Tech/M.Arch/M.Plan (CCMT) is a single-window application portal for 31 NITs, IIEST Shibpur, and top IIITs.

  • Single centralized choice filling of hundreds of program-college combinations.
  • Choices locked prior to Round 1.
  • Allotment across Regular Rounds 1, 2, and 3.
  • Option to Freeze, Slide, or Float allotted seats.
  • Special Rounds (SR 1, SR 2) and National Spot Round (NSR) for vacant seats.
Step 1 · March

IIT Applications

Fill separate online M.Tech application forms on individual IIT portals (IITB, IITD, IITM, IITK, etc.) using your GATE roll number.

Step 2 · April

Register on COAP

Create your candidate login on the COAP portal using your GATE registration number. Enter the exact same email and mobile used on IIT portals.

Step 3 · May

COAP Main Rounds 1 to 5

Offers released in synchronized rounds. Log in during the designated 48-hour window and choose Accept & Freeze, Retain & Wait, or Reject & Wait.

Step 4 · June

CCMT Choice Locking

Simultaneously register on ccmt.admissions.nic.in. Upload qualifying degree and category certificates, pay registration fees, and lock choices.

Step 5 · July

Seat Acceptance & Reporting

Pay Seat Acceptance Fee (SAF), complete online document verification, download provisional allotment letter, and physically report to campus.

🎯 The 28-Mark Foundation

General Aptitude (15 marks) + Engineering Mathematics (13 marks) constitute roughly 28% of the total examination. Scoring 24+ marks in this section provides an unbeatable safety cushion for high All India Ranks.

📚 20-Year PYQ Mastery

Solve every single Past Year Question (PYQ) from the last 15 to 20 years at least 3 times. GATE questions frequently test identical mathematical and logical concepts with modified parameters.

💻 Mock Test Calibration

Attempt at least 25 to 30 full-length simulated mock tests on actual CBT interfaces. Strictly practice with the on-screen virtual calculator to build speed and eliminate manual typing errors.

GATE (Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering) is a national entrance examination conducted jointly by the Indian Institute of Science (IISc Bangalore) and seven Indian Institutes of Technology (IIT Bombay, IIT Delhi, IIT Guwahati, IIT Kanpur, IIT Kharagpur, IIT Madras, and IIT Roorkee) on behalf of the National Coordination Board (NCB) - GATE, Department of Higher Education, Ministry of Education, Government of India. The organizing institute rotates among these 8 premier institutions annually.

A candidate who is currently studying in the 3rd or higher year of any undergraduate degree program in Engineering, Technology, Architecture, Science, Commerce, or Arts, or who has already completed any government-approved degree program, is fully eligible to register and appear for GATE 2027. There is no minimum or maximum age limit, and candidates can take the exam an unlimited number of times.
GATE Raw Marks represent the actual positive score minus negative deductions scored by a candidate out of 100 in their test paper. The GATE Score is a normalized, statistically calibrated metric on a scale of 0 to 1000 computed using a multi-year percentile normalization formula. For single-shift papers, raw marks are directly transformed into the GATE score. For multi-shift papers (e.g., CS, ME, CE), normalization accounts for variance in test difficulty across shifts. IIT M.Tech cutoffs and COAP offers are decided primarily on the GATE Score (out of 1000).
For academic admissions (M.Tech, M.S. by Research, and Direct Ph.D. at IISc, IITs, NITs, and IIITs), the GATE scorecard is legally valid for three years from the date of result announcement. However, for PSU recruitment (Public Sector Undertakings such as ONGC, IOCL, NTPC, HPCL, BHEL, BARC, DRDO), almost all recruiting organizations strictly mandate a valid score from the current recruitment year only.
Yes. Candidates are permitted to appear in either ONE or TWO test papers. If appearing in two papers, the primary paper can be chosen freely from any of the 30 papers, but the secondary paper MUST be chosen strictly from the pre-approved Two-Paper Combination List published by the GATE organizing committee (for example, CS can pair with DA or MA; EC can pair with EE, IN, DA, or PH; ME can pair with AE, NM, PI, or XE). An additional application fee applies for the second paper.
For Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ): 1/3 mark is deducted for each incorrect 1-mark question, and 2/3 mark is deducted for each incorrect 2-mark question. For Multiple Select Questions (MSQ) and Numerical Answer Type (NAT) questions, there is ZERO negative marking. In MSQs, there is no partial credit: you must select all correct choices and no incorrect ones to get marks.
COAP is NOT an application portal. Candidates must apply individually to each participating IIT (e.g., IIT Bombay, IIT Madras). COAP provides a centralized platform where all participating IITs upload their admission offers for registered candidates. Candidates log in to COAP during each offer round and choose from three options: 'Accept & Freeze' (accept offer and exit counselling), 'Retain & Wait' (hold current best offer and participate in subsequent rounds for higher choices; max twice on the same offer), or 'Reject & Wait' (reject all offers in round and wait for higher rounds).
CCMT (Centralized Counselling for M.Tech/M.Arch/M.Plan) is a unified admission portal for 31 National Institutes of Technology (NITs), IIEST Shibpur, leading IIITs, and other Government-Funded Technical Institutes (GFTIs). Unlike COAP, candidates submit a single centralized application on ccmt.admissions.nic.in, fill and lock their program-institute preferences, and receive seat allotments across Regular Rounds 1, 2, 3, followed by Special Rounds (SR1, SR2) and National Spot Rounds (NSR).
