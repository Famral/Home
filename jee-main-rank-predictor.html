Understanding JEE Main Score Calculation & Admission Process

JEE Main (Joint Entrance Examination - Mains) is conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA) twice a year (Session 1 in January/February and Session 2 in April). It tests Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics, with the best of the two session percentile scores considered for ranking. In 2024, approximately 14.15 lakh unique candidates appeared, making it the largest engineering entrance exam in India by participation.

JEE Main Exam Structure & Scoring (Official – NTA)

Total Score (out of 300) = Physics (100) + Chemistry (100) + Mathematics (100)
  • Each subject: 20 MCQ questions (+4/-1) + 10 Numerical Value questions (+4/0) = 100 marks
  • MCQ: +4 marks for correct answer, -1 mark for wrong answer, 0 for unattempted
  • Numerical Value (Integer type): +4 marks for correct, 0 for wrong (no negative marking)
  • Total: 90 questions; duration: 3 hours (180 minutes)
  • Best of two session percentile scores is used for final All India Rank (AIR)

Key Factors Influencing Your JEE Main Rank

  • Percentile-Based Rank (Normalization): JEE Main uses a percentile normalization process across multiple sessions and shifts to ensure fairness. Your raw score is converted to a percentile, and the best percentile across sessions determines your final AIR. Candidates are not compared by raw marks directly.
  • All Three Subjects Carry Equal Weightage: Unlike some state exams, all three subjects in JEE Main carry equal marks (100 each). However, Mathematics is typically the differentiating factor at high percentiles as fewer candidates score perfectly in it.
  • Negative Marking in MCQs: JEE Main MCQ questions carry -1 penalty per wrong answer. Numerical value questions have no negative marking. Candidates must be careful about guessing on MCQs to avoid score reduction.
  • Category-Based Reservations (Central): JEE Main follows central government reservation policies: EWS 10%, OBC-NCL 27%, SC 15%, ST 7.5%, PwD 5% horizontal. Separate merit lists are prepared for each category by JoSAA for NIT/IIIT/GFTI admissions.
  • Tiebreaker Rules (Official NTA): In case of equal percentile, tiebreaker order is: 1) Higher NTA Percentile in Mathematics, 2) Higher NTA Percentile in Physics, 3) Higher NTA Percentile in Chemistry, 4) Candidates older in age (earlier date of birth) are preferred.

JEE Main Admission Process & Eligibility Rules

Summary of the JEE Main admission structure, eligibility requirements, scoring, and key JoSAA counselling rules for NIT, IIIT, and GFTI admissions.

Eligibility & Score Calculation

  • Eligibility: Passed Class 12 (or equivalent) with Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics as compulsory subjects. Minimum 75% aggregate in PCM for General/EWS/OBC-NCL (65% for SC/ST) for NIT/IIIT/GFTI admission. No age limit from 2021 onwards (NTA policy).
  • Score Formula: Total JEE Main Score = Physics (100) + Chemistry (100) + Mathematics (100) = 300. MCQ: +4/-1. Numerical Type: +4/0. Best of two sessions is taken for AIR.
  • Percentile-Based AIR: NTA converts raw score to a percentile via normalization. The best percentile from either session forms your final AIR, used by JoSAA and CSAB for seat allocation in NITs, IIITs, and GFTIs.

JoSAA Counselling & Seat Allotment

  • JoSAA (Joint Seat Allocation Authority): JEE Main qualified candidates participate in JoSAA counselling for NITs, IIITs, and GFTIs online at josaa.nic.in. JoSAA conducts 6 rounds of seat allotment. JEE Advanced qualified candidates additionally compete for IIT seats.
  • Home State / Other State Quota: NITs allocate 50% seats to Home State (HS) candidates and 50% to Other State (OS) candidates. Cutoff ranks differ significantly between HS and OS. IIITs and GFTIs generally have All India quota.
  • 31 NITs, 26 IIITs, 33+ GFTIs: JoSAA 2024 covered 31 NITs, 26 IIITs, and 33+ GFTIs offering approximately 56,000+ undergraduate engineering seats across disciplines all over India.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the JEE Main rank (AIR) calculated?
JEE Main All India Rank (AIR) is calculated based on the NTA Percentile Score. The percentile is computed using the formula: Percentile = (100 x Number of candidates who scored equal to or less than you) / Total candidates appeared. The best percentile across both sessions is taken as your final score, and AIR is assigned in descending order of percentile. In case of equal percentile, tie-breaking is applied: first by Mathematics percentile, then Physics, then Chemistry, then date of birth (older candidate preferred).
What JEE Main percentile is needed for a rank under 1,000?
To secure a JEE Main AIR under 1,000 (General category), a candidate typically needs a percentile of 99.93+. In JEE Main 2024, with approximately 14.15 lakh unique candidates, using the NTA formula: Rank = (100 - 99.93) x 14,15,110 / 100 = approx. 990 candidates ahead. This percentile typically corresponds to approximately 250+ marks out of 300, though the exact marks-to-percentile mapping varies by session difficulty.
What are the official JEE Main 2024 qualifying cutoffs for JEE Advanced?
The NTA officially released these JEE Main 2024 qualifying percentile cutoffs (for JEE Advanced 2024 eligibility): General: 93.24 percentile, EWS: 81.33 percentile, OBC-NCL: 79.68 percentile, SC: 60.09 percentile, ST: 46.70 percentile, PwD: 0.0018 percentile. These cutoffs allow the top ~2.5 lakh candidates to appear for JEE Advanced. Note: These are not NIT/IIIT admission cutoffs, which are decided by JoSAA separately based on available seats.
How many times can I appear for JEE Main?
As per current NTA rules, a candidate can appear for JEE Main a maximum of 3 consecutive years after passing Class 12. Each year, JEE Main is conducted twice (Session 1 in January/February and Session 2 in April). The best NTA Percentile Score across both sessions in a given year is considered. The best percentile across all eligible attempts is used for ranking.
Is there negative marking in JEE Main?
Yes, JEE Main has negative marking for MCQ questions only. Each correct MCQ answer earns +4 marks and each wrong MCQ answer deducts -1 mark. For Numerical Value (integer type) questions, there is no negative marking - a correct answer earns +4 marks and a wrong answer earns 0. Unattempted questions earn 0 marks regardless of type. There are 20 MCQ and 10 Numerical Value questions per subject.
What is the difference between Home State and Other State quota in NITs?
In NITs, 50% of seats are allocated as Home State (HS) quota for candidates who are domicile residents of the state where the NIT is located, and 50% as Other State (OS) quota for candidates from other states. HS quota typically has more lenient closing ranks than OS quota. For example, at NIT Trichy for CSE, the HS (Tamil Nadu) closing rank may be around 1,500 while the OS closing rank could be around 2,100 (General category). IIITs and GFTIs in JoSAA generally have All India quota without HS/OS distinction.
