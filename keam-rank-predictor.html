Understanding KEAM Score Calculation & Admission Process

KEAM (Kerala Engineering Architecture Medical) is conducted by the Commissioner for Entrance Examinations (CEE), Government of Kerala. The exam consists of two papers: Paper 1 (Physics & Chemistry) and Paper 2 (Mathematics), each with 120 questions carrying 4 marks each and negative marking of −1 per wrong answer. Approximately 1.2 to 1.5 lakh candidates appear for KEAM each year, competing for seats in over 160 colleges across Kerala.

KEAM Exam Structure & Scoring (Official – CEE, Kerala)

Total Score (out of 960) = Paper 1 (Physics + Chemistry) + Paper 2 (Mathematics)
  • Paper 1 – Physics: 60 questions × 4 marks = 240 marks
  • Paper 1 – Chemistry: 60 questions × 4 marks = 240 marks
  • Paper 2 – Mathematics: 120 questions × 4 marks = 480 marks
  • Negative marking: −1 mark for each wrong answer in all papers

Key Factors Influencing Your KEAM Rank

  • Mathematics has the Highest Weightage: Paper 2 (Mathematics) carries 480 marks, which is 50% of the total KEAM score of 960. Physics and Chemistry together contribute 480 marks (240 each). Excelling in Mathematics is the single most impactful factor in improving your KEAM score.
  • Entrance Exam + Class 12 Board Marks: For engineering seat allotment, CEE Kerala uses a combined rank based on 50% weightage to KEAM exam score and 50% weightage to normalized Class 12 PCM marks. Strong board marks can compensate for a slightly lower exam score.
  • Category-Based Reservations: Kerala follows state government reservation policies. Categories include General, SEBC (27%), SC (8%), ST (2%), OEC (3%), EWS (10%), and various special quotas for PWD, NRI, Sports, Ex-Servicemen, etc. Separate rank lists are prepared for each category.
  • Negative Marking Impact: KEAM deducts 1 mark for every wrong answer. Strategic answering by skipping highly uncertain questions can meaningfully improve your net score and thus your rank.
  • Tiebreaker Rules (Official): In case of equal KEAM combined scores, the tiebreaker order is: 1) Higher marks in Mathematics (Paper 2), 2) Higher marks in Physics, 3) Higher marks in Chemistry, 4) Higher Class 12 PCM aggregate, 5) Date of birth (elder candidate preferred).

KEAM Admission Process & Eligibility Rules

Summary of the KEAM admission structure, eligibility requirements, score calculation, and key counselling rules for Kerala engineering college admissions through CEE Kerala.

Eligibility & Score Calculation

  • Eligibility: Passed Class 12 (or equivalent) with Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics as compulsory subjects. Minimum 45% aggregate in PCM for General category (40% for SEBC, lower for SC/ST).
  • Score Formula: Total KEAM Score = Paper 1 (Physics 240 + Chemistry 240) + Paper 2 (Mathematics 480) = 960 marks. Negative marking of −1 per wrong answer applies in all papers.
  • Combined Rank: For seat allotment, 50% weightage to KEAM exam score and 50% to normalized Class 12 PCM marks are used to prepare the final combined rank list for engineering admissions.

Counselling & Seat Allotment

  • Centralized Allotment Process (CAP): KEAM counselling is conducted by CEE Kerala through a centralized online process. Candidates fill college and branch choices at cee.kerala.gov.in.
  • Category-Wise Seats: Kerala government reservation: SEBC 27%, SC 8%, ST 2%, OEC 3%, EWS 10%, plus special reservations for PWD, NRI, Sports, Ex-Servicemen, etc. Separate rank lists are drawn for each category.
  • 160+ Colleges: Over 160 government, government-aided, and private engineering colleges across Kerala participate in KEAM counselling, offering approximately 60,000+ undergraduate engineering seats.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the KEAM rank calculated?
KEAM rank is calculated based on the combined score from the KEAM entrance exam (960 marks total from Paper 1 and Paper 2) and Class 12 board marks. For the final engineering seat allotment rank, CEE Kerala assigns 50% weightage to KEAM exam score and 50% weightage to normalized Class 12 PCM marks. The KEAM exam itself consists of Paper 1 (Physics 240 + Chemistry 240 = 480 marks) and Paper 2 (Mathematics 480 marks), with negative marking of −1 per wrong answer. Separate rank lists are prepared for each category (General, SEBC, SC, ST, OEC, etc.).
What KEAM score is needed for a rank under 1,000?
To secure a KEAM rank under 1,000 (General category), a candidate typically needs a total KEAM score of approximately 800+ out of 960. In KEAM 2024, the top 1,000 ranks were dominated by candidates scoring above 780 in the entrance exam. Combined with strong Class 12 PCM marks (90%+ recommended), even a score of 750+ in the exam can push a candidate into the top 1,000 combined rank range.
What are the category reservations in KEAM and how do they affect admissions?
KEAM follows Kerala government reservation policies. The categories are: General (open seats), SEBC (Socially and Educationally Backward Classes — 27%), SC (Scheduled Caste — 8%), ST (Scheduled Tribe — 2%), OEC (Other Eligible Communities — 3%), EWS (Economically Weaker Section — 10%), and special reservations for PWD, NRI, Sports, Ex-Servicemen, etc. Each category has a separate rank list with reserved seats, so the effective cutoff rank for admission is more lenient for reserved categories compared to General.
How does negative marking affect KEAM score and rank?
KEAM applies negative marking of −1 mark for every wrong answer in both Paper 1 (Physics & Chemistry) and Paper 2 (Mathematics). Unattempted questions carry zero marks. This means a wrong answer effectively costs you 5 marks (you lose 1 instead of gaining 4). Strategic answering by skipping highly uncertain questions can meaningfully improve your net score and rank. On average, leaving a question unanswered is better than guessing randomly when your confidence is below 25%.
How many candidates appear for KEAM each year?
Approximately 1.2 to 1.5 lakh candidates appear for KEAM engineering entrance exam each year. Kerala is one of the most competitive state-level engineering entrance processes in South India, with over 160 participating colleges and approximately 60,000+ undergraduate engineering seats. The number of candidates has grown steadily with increasing awareness and participation from students across Kerala and neighbouring states.
Can students from other states appear for KEAM?
Yes, students from other states can appear for KEAM. However, state quota seats (the bulk of seats in Government and Aided colleges) are reserved preferentially for Kerala domicile students. Non-Kerala candidates can compete for the Management Quota or NRI Quota seats in private colleges, which are not part of the CEE centralized allotment. Kerala domicile is verified at the time of counselling, and candidates must provide appropriate domicile certificates.
