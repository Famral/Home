CUET Percentile Formula & University Cutoff Explained

NTA uses the Equipercentile Normalization Method to calculate the CUET UG Percentile Score, ensuring fairness across multiple shifts and different paper difficulty levels:

Official NTA Percentile Formula

The NTA Percentile Score is calculated within each subject and shift using:

Percentile = (100 × N≤) / N_total
  • N≤: Number of candidates who scored equal to or less than the candidate in that specific subject/shift.
  • N_total: Total number of candidates who appeared in that specific subject/shift.
  • Multi-Shift Normalization: Raw marks within each shift are normalised to ensure fairness before final percentile computation.
  • Subject-wise Percentile: Each subject (General Test, Language, Domain) has its own separate percentile score used by universities.

CUET UG 2026 Exam Pattern & Marking Scheme

CUET UG 2026 is conducted as a Computer-Based Test (CBT) by NTA. The exam is divided into three sections: Language Test (Section IA/IB), Domain-Specific Subjects (Section II), and General Test (Section III):

Overall Exam Structure

  • Section IA (Language): 13 Indian languages — 50 MCQs, 45 minutes, 200 marks
  • Section IB (Foreign Lang.): English + 19 other languages — 45 minutes, 200 marks
  • Section II (Domain): Up to 6 domain subjects — 50 MCQs each, 45 min, 200 marks each
  • Section III (General Test): 75 MCQs (attempt 60), 60 minutes, 200 marks

Marking Scheme

  • Correct Answer: +5 marks per correct MCQ
  • Incorrect Answer: −1 mark (negative marking applies)
  • Unattempted: 0 marks — no penalty for skipping
  • Maximum Score: 200 marks per subject/section (5 × 40 attempted Qs)

General Test Syllabus

  • General Knowledge: Current Affairs, Static GK, Science & Technology
  • Quantitative Reasoning: Basic Maths, Data Interpretation, Arithmetic
  • Logical & Analytical Reasoning: Series, Coding-Decoding, Puzzles
  • Language Comprehension: Reading passages & vocabulary (English)

Qualifying Criteria & University Admission

  • Central Universities: 250+ universities including DU, BHU, JNU, AMU use CUET scores for UG admission
  • University-Specific Merit: Each university prepares its own merit list using specified subject combinations
  • Category Reservation: OBC-NCL (27%), SC (15%), ST (7.5%), EWS (10%), PwD (5% horizontal)
  • Score Validity: Valid for current academic year only; cannot be carried forward

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How does the CUET Percentile Predictor calculate my NTA Percentile & All India Rank?
The CUET UG Percentile Predictor applies the NTA Equipercentile Normalization Method: Percentile = (100 × Candidates scoring ≤ you) / Total candidates in that subject/shift. The estimated All India Rank is derived from historical percentile-to-rank conversion data calibrated with actual NTA results across CUET UG 2022–2025, accounting for a total candidate pool of approximately 14–15 lakh students.
What is the key difference between CUET Marks, NTA Percentile Score, and All India Rank?
CUET Marks are the raw marks obtained in each subject (out of 200 for General Test and most domain subjects). The NTA Percentile Score (0–100) reflects the percentage of candidates who scored equal to or less than you in that specific subject/shift — it is a relative score, not an absolute one. The All India Rank is your final merit standing used by universities to prepare admission merit lists.
What CUET percentile is required for Delhi University (DU) top colleges?
For popular programmes (B.Com Hons., B.A. Hons. Economics, B.Sc. Hons.) at top DU colleges like SRCC, Miranda House, and Hindu College, General category candidates typically require a CUET percentile of 99.2–99.9+ in the relevant domain subject and General Test. Commerce and Science streams at SRCC and LSR are among the most competitive, while some Humanities programmes at newer DU colleges have cutoffs in the 90–95 percentile range.
How does CUET marks normalisation work across multiple shifts?
NTA uses the Equipercentile Normalisation Method for CUET UG. Since the exam is conducted in multiple sessions and shifts across different dates, raw marks within each shift are converted to a percentile score relative to all candidates who appeared in that specific subject across all shifts. This ensures fairness across varying paper difficulty levels and is why two candidates with the same raw marks may receive different percentile scores if they appeared in different shifts.
How many subjects should I attempt in CUET UG 2026?
CUET UG 2026 allows candidates to appear in a Language Test (Section IA/IB), up to 6 Domain-Specific Subjects (Section II), and the General Test (Section III). You should attempt subjects as required by your target universities. Most central universities specify a combination of 2–3 domain subjects plus the General Test for their UG programmes. Check each university's CUET subject requirements carefully before registering.
What is the negative marking rule in CUET UG 2026?
In CUET UG 2026, each correct answer carries +5 marks and each incorrect answer carries −1 mark (negative marking). Unanswered questions carry 0 marks — there is no penalty for leaving questions blank. In the General Test, candidates must attempt 60 out of 75 questions (any 60 of their choice), so strategic selection of questions is recommended.
