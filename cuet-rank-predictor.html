Understanding CUET UG Score Calculation & Admission Process

CUET UG (Common University Entrance Test – Undergraduate) is conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA) on behalf of the University Grants Commission (UGC). CUET replaces individual university entrance exams and Class 12 percentage-based admissions for over 300 central, state, deemed, and private universities. Approximately 13.47 lakh candidates registered and 11.13 lakh appeared for CUET UG 2024, making it one of India’s largest undergraduate entrance examinations.

CUET UG Exam Structure & Scoring (Official – NTA)

Marking Scheme (All Sections): +5 (Correct) | −1 (Incorrect) | 0 (Unattempted)
  • Section IA & IB (Languages): Attempt 40 out of 50 questions — Maximum 200 marks per language. Duration: 45 minutes per language.
  • Section II (Domain-Specific Subjects): Attempt 40 out of 50 questions — Maximum 200 marks per subject. Up to 6 domain subjects. Duration: 45–60 minutes (60 min for Maths, Physics, Chemistry, Accountancy).
  • Section III (General Test): Attempt 50 out of 60 questions — Maximum 250 marks. Covers GK, Current Affairs, Mental Ability, Quantitative Aptitude & Logical Reasoning. Duration: 60 minutes.
  • Scores are normalized using the equi-percentile method (NTA Normalization) across multiple exam shifts for fairness.
  • Universities prepare their own merit lists using normalized NTA scores as per their Bulletin of Information (BoI).

Key Factors Influencing Your CUET Admission

  • No Centralized All India Rank: Unlike JEE or NEET, NTA does not release a centralized All India Rank for CUET UG. Each participating university independently prepares its own program-wise merit lists using the normalized NTA scores. Your score is evaluated separately for each university you apply to.
  • NTA Normalization — Equi-Percentile Method: Since CUET is conducted across multiple shifts with varying difficulty levels, raw marks are converted into normalized NTA scores using the equi-percentile method. All universities use these normalized scores for admission, not raw marks.
  • Course-Specific Subject Requirements: Each program at each university specifies which CUET subjects count toward the merit score. Check the Bulletin of Information (BoI) of each university for exact requirements (e.g., DU B.Com Hons. uses Accountancy + English + elective; B.Sc. Physics uses Physics + Chemistry + Maths).
  • Category-Based Reservations (Central Govt. Norms): Central universities follow Central Government reservation norms: OBC-NCL (27%), SC (15%), ST (7.5%), EWS (10%), and PwD (5% horizontal reservation). Separate merit lists are prepared for each category with significantly lower effective cutoffs for reserved categories.
  • Class 12 Marks Not Used for Merit (DU): Delhi University does not use Class 12 marks for merit calculation; they are only used as a tiebreaker when two candidates have identical CUET scores. Other universities may have different policies — always check official BoI.
  • DU CSAS — Common Seat Allocation System: Delhi University admissions are managed through the DU CSAS portal (admission.uod.ac.in). After CUET results, candidates register on CSAS, fill program-college preferences, and participate in centralized seat allocation rounds including mop-up rounds.

CUET UG Admission Process & Eligibility Rules

Summary of the CUET UG admission structure, eligibility requirements, score calculation, and key counselling rules for central university undergraduate admissions through NTA.

Eligibility & Score Calculation

  • Eligibility: Passed or appearing in Class 12 (10+2) from a recognized board. Minimum percentage criteria vary by university and program. Generally 50% aggregate for General (45% for SC/ST). Check individual university BoI for exact eligibility criteria.
  • Score Formula: Raw Score = (+5 × Correct) + (−1 × Incorrect) + (0 × Unattempted). Domain/Language max: 200 marks (40 ques). General Test max: 250 marks (50 ques). NTA then normalizes raw scores across shifts using equi-percentile method.
  • NTA Normalization: Raw scores are normalized by NTA using the equi-percentile method across exam shifts. The resulting NTA Score (normalized) is used by all universities for merit list preparation. Class 12 marks are not included in the CUET merit score (policy varies by university).

Admission Process & Seat Allotment

  • University-Wise Admission: Each university independently conducts admissions using CUET scores. For DU, admissions are through the CSAS portal (admission.uod.ac.in). For BHU, AMU, JMI and others, candidates apply separately on each university’s official portal after CUET results.
  • Category-Wise Seats (Central Govt.): OBC-NCL (27%), SC (15%), ST (7.5%), EWS (10%), PwD (5% horizontal reservation across categories). Separate merit lists are drawn for each category, with category-specific cutoffs significantly lower than General cutoffs.
  • 300+ Universities Accepting CUET: CUET UG 2024 was accepted by over 300 central, state, deemed, and private universities including all 45 Central Universities, 13 State Universities, and numerous deemed institutions, offering lakhs of UG seats across India.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the CUET UG NTA score calculated?
CUET UG uses a marking scheme of +5 marks for correct answers and −1 mark for incorrect answers. Unattempted questions carry 0 marks. The raw score is calculated as: (Correct × 5) − (Incorrect × 1). Since CUET is conducted in multiple shifts with varying difficulty levels, NTA converts raw marks into normalized NTA scores using the equi-percentile method to ensure fairness. This normalized score is what appears on your CUET scorecard and is used by all universities for admission.
What CUET score is needed for Delhi University top colleges?
For top Delhi University colleges like SRCC, Miranda House, Hindu College, and Ramjas, competitive CUET scores for the most popular programs (B.Com Hons., B.A. Hons. Economics, B.Sc. Hons.) typically require 190+ out of 200 in relevant domain subjects for General category. DU uses the CSAS portal for centralized seat allocation, and the merit score is the combined normalized NTA score across subjects as specified by each program’s Bulletin of Information.
Does CUET UG have a centralized All India Rank?
No, CUET UG does not have a centralized All India Rank (AIR) like JEE Main or NEET. NTA provides normalized NTA Scores to candidates on their scorecards. Each participating university independently prepares its own program-wise merit lists based on these NTA scores. Your admission eligibility is determined separately by each university based on their specific subject and score requirements as stated in their official Bulletin of Information.
Does CUET UG have negative marking?
Yes, CUET UG has negative marking of −1 mark for every incorrect answer. Each correct answer earns +5 marks, and unattempted questions carry 0 marks. Candidates should carefully balance attempting questions — guessing can cost marks. The maximum raw score per domain subject is 200 marks (40 questions × 5 marks) and for the General Test is 250 marks (50 questions × 5 marks).
How many candidates appear for CUET UG each year?
CUET UG is one of India’s largest undergraduate entrance exams. In 2024, approximately 13.47 lakh candidates registered and 11.13 lakh appeared for the examination. CUET UG is accepted by 300+ universities including all 45 Central Universities, numerous state universities, and deemed institutions, offering access to thousands of undergraduate programs across science, arts, commerce, and professional streams.
What is the NTA equi-percentile normalization in CUET UG?
CUET UG is conducted across multiple days and shifts with different question papers. To ensure fairness, NTA applies the equi-percentile normalization method. This process maps the percentile of each raw score in one shift to the corresponding score in a base shift. The resulting normalized NTA Score is on a common scale, allowing universities to compare candidates fairly regardless of which shift they appeared in. This normalized score is displayed on your CUET scorecard and is used for all admission purposes.
