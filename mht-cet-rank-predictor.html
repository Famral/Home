Understanding MHT-CET Score Calculation & Admission Process

MHT-CET (Maharashtra Common Entrance Test) is conducted by the State CET Cell, Government of Maharashtra. The PCM group exam tests Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics. Mathematics carries double weightage (2 marks per question) while Physics and Chemistry carry 1 or 2 marks per question depending on difficulty group. Approximately 4 to 5 lakh candidates appear for MHT-CET PCM each year, competing for seats in over 300 colleges across Maharashtra.

MHT-CET Exam Structure & Scoring (Official – State CET Cell, Maharashtra)

Total Score (out of 200) = Physics (50) + Chemistry (50) + Mathematics (100)
  • Physics: ~10 questions (2 marks each) + ~10 questions (1 mark each) = scaled to 50 marks
  • Chemistry: ~10 questions (2 marks each) + ~10 questions (1 mark each) = scaled to 50 marks
  • Mathematics: ~10 questions (2 marks each) + ~10 questions (1 mark each) = scaled to 100 marks
  • No negative marking in MHT-CET PCM group
  • Percentile is calculated per subject and a combined percentile determines rank

Key Factors Influencing Your MHT-CET Rank

  • Mathematics has the Highest Weightage: Mathematics carries 100 out of 200 total marks (50% of total MHT-CET PCM score). Physics and Chemistry together contribute 100 marks (50 each). Excelling in Mathematics is the single most impactful factor in improving your MHT-CET score and rank.
  • Percentile-Based Rank (Normalization): MHT-CET uses a percentile normalization process across multiple sessions to ensure fairness. Your score is converted to a percentile, and the combined percentile determines the final merit rank used for CAP (Centralized Admission Process) rounds.
  • Category-Based Reservations: Maharashtra follows state government reservation policies. Categories include Open, OBC (19%), SC (13%), ST (7%), VJ/DT (3%), NT-B (2.5%), NT-C (3.5%), NT-D (2%), SBC (2%), EWS (10%), and special quotas for PWD, NRI, etc. Separate merit lists are drawn for each category.
  • No Negative Marking: Unlike many other entrance exams, MHT-CET PCM group has no negative marking. Candidates should attempt all questions, as unanswered questions simply earn zero marks without penalty.
  • Tiebreaker Rules (Official): In case of equal MHT-CET percentile scores, the tiebreaker order is: 1) Higher percentile in Mathematics, 2) Higher percentile in Physics, 3) Higher percentile in Chemistry, 4) Higher Class 12 PCM aggregate, 5) Date of birth (elder candidate preferred).

MHT-CET Admission Process & Eligibility Rules

Summary of the MHT-CET admission structure, eligibility requirements, score calculation, and key counselling rules for Maharashtra engineering college admissions through State CET Cell.

Eligibility & Score Calculation

  • Eligibility: Passed Class 12 (or equivalent) with Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics as compulsory subjects. Minimum 45% aggregate in PCM for Open category (40% for OBC, lower for SC/ST). Must be an Indian citizen.
  • Score Formula: Total MHT-CET Score = Physics (50) + Chemistry (50) + Mathematics (100) = 200 marks. No negative marking in PCM group. Percentile score is computed per subject across all sessions.
  • Percentile-Based Rank: The combined percentile of PCM (with Mathematics double-weighted) is used to prepare the final merit list for CAP. Your rank is determined from the combined percentile across all test-takers in the same group.

CAP Counselling & Seat Allotment

  • Centralized Admission Process (CAP): MHT-CET counselling is conducted by State CET Cell Maharashtra through online centralized process. Candidates fill college and branch choices at cetcell.mahacet.org. Multiple CAP rounds are held.
  • Category-Wise Seats: Maharashtra government reservation: OBC 19%, SC 13%, ST 7%, VJ/DT 3%, NT-B 2.5%, NT-C 3.5%, NT-D 2%, SBC 2%, EWS 10%, plus special reservations for PWD, NRI, etc. Separate merit lists for each category.
  • 300+ Colleges: Over 300 government, government-aided, and private engineering colleges across Maharashtra participate in MHT-CET CAP rounds, offering approximately 1.5 lakh+ undergraduate engineering seats.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the MHT-CET rank calculated?
MHT-CET rank is calculated based on the percentile score in Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics (PCM group). Percentile is computed separately for each subject across all sessions and a combined percentile — with Mathematics weighted double — is used to determine the final merit rank. For Engineering admissions, only the PCM group percentile is considered. Separate merit lists are prepared for each category (Open, OBC, SC, ST, etc.).
What MHT-CET percentile is needed for a rank under 1,000?
To secure a MHT-CET rank under 1,000 (Open category), a candidate typically needs a percentile of 99.95+. In MHT-CET 2024, the top 1,000 ranks were dominated by candidates scoring 155+ out of 200 in the PCM group. Even a score of 148+ with excellent Maths performance can place a candidate in the top 1,000 rank range.
What are the category reservations in MHT-CET and how do they affect admissions?
MHT-CET follows Maharashtra government reservation policies. The categories are: Open (general seats), OBC (19%), SC (13%), ST (7%), VJ/DT (3%), NT-B/C/D (2.5%, 3.5%, 2%), SBC (2%), EWS (10%), and special reservations for PWD, NRI, Sports, etc. Each category has a separate merit list so effective cutoff ranks are more lenient for reserved categories.
Is there negative marking in MHT-CET?
No, MHT-CET PCM group has no negative marking. Each correct answer earns the designated marks (2 marks for 2-mark questions, 1 mark for 1-mark questions) and unanswered questions earn zero marks. This is a significant advantage compared to JEE Main and many other entrance exams. Candidates should attempt all questions to maximize their score.
How many candidates appear for MHT-CET each year?
Approximately 4 to 5 lakh candidates appear for MHT-CET PCM group each year. Maharashtra is one of the largest state-level engineering entrance processes in India, with over 300 participating colleges and approximately 1.5 lakh+ undergraduate engineering seats available. The exam is conducted in multiple sessions across several days using a Computer Based Test (CBT) format.
Can students from other states appear for MHT-CET?
Yes, students from other states can appear for MHT-CET. However, state quota seats (the bulk of seats in Government and Aided colleges) are reserved preferentially for Maharashtra domicile students. Non-Maharashtra candidates can compete for the All India Quota (AIQ) or Management Quota or NRI Quota seats in private colleges. Maharashtra domicile is verified at the time of CAP rounds, and candidates must provide appropriate domicile certificates.
