B.Arch 50:50 Composite Merit Formula & Admission Rules Explained

Admissions to 5-year Bachelor of Architecture (B.Arch) programmes in India follow statutory Council of Architecture (CoA) guidelines combining your NATA entrance score with Class 12th / Diploma Board marks:

50:50 Composite Merit Calculation

Most state counselling authorities (such as Maharashtra State CET Cell CAP, Karnataka KEA, JAC Delhi, and TNEA) compute B.Arch merit rank using the standard 50:50 weightage formula:

Composite Score (out of 200) = NATA Score (out of 200) × 0.5 + Board Percentage (%) × 1.0
  • NATA Component (50% Weightage): Your NATA score out of 200 is divided by 2 to yield a score out of 100.
  • Board Component (50% Weightage): Your overall Class 12th / 10+3 Diploma percentage is calculated out of 100.
  • Eligibility Criteria: Candidates must have passed 10+2 with Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics (PCM) or 10+3 Diploma with Mathematics with minimum 50% aggregate marks.

NATA 2026 Exam Pattern & Marking Scheme

NATA is administered by the Council of Architecture (CoA) as a comprehensive 180-minute (3 Hours) evaluation comprising Part A (Offline Drawing / Visual Composition) and Part B (Computer Based Test MCQs) totaling 200 marks:

Exam Structure & Marks

  • Total Duration: 180 Minutes (3 Hours Total)
  • Total Maximum Marks: 200 Marks
  • Negative Marking: NO Negative Marking in any section
  • Overall Qualifying Cutoff: Minimum 70/200 aggregate marks

Part A: Drawing & Composition (80 M)

  • Mode: Offline Paper-and-Pencil Drawing Test (90 mins)
  • Drawing & Sketching: Composition, Perspective & 3D visualization
  • Visual Aesthetics: Color harmony, light, shadow & scale proportions
  • Sectional Cutoff: Minimum ~20/80 qualifying threshold

Part B: Online Aptitude (120 M)

  • Mode: Online Computer-Based Test (90 mins)
  • General Aptitude & Reasoning: Visual-spatial reasoning & architectural awareness
  • Mathematics & Logic: Applied basic mathematics & analytical logic
  • Sectional Cutoff: Minimum ~30/120 qualifying threshold

CoA Qualifying Benchmarks

  • Scorecard Validity: Valid for 2 academic years
  • Multiple Attempts: Candidates can take up to 3 tests per year
  • Best Score Rule: Best of all valid attempts considered
  • Centralized Validity: Recognized across all CoA-approved B.Arch colleges

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How does the NATA Rank Predictor estimate my All India Rank and Percentile?
The NATA Rank Predictor evaluates your raw exam score out of 200 marks and models it against historical Council of Architecture (CoA) mark-to-rank trends and percentile curves from over 45,000+ annual test-takers across all test sessions. It computes your estimated All India Rank range, percentile, and 50:50 composite merit score with Class 12th Board marks.
What is the 50:50 Composite Merit Formula used in B.Arch admissions?
In most central and state admission processes (such as Maharashtra CAP, Karnataka KEA, JAC Delhi, and TNEA), merit ranks are determined using an equal 50:50 weightage: 50% from your NATA score (converted out of 100) and 50% from your qualifying Class 12th / 10+3 Diploma aggregate percentage. The sum yields your Composite Merit Score out of 200.
What is considered a safe score in NATA for Sir JJ College, CEPT, and RVCA?
For top-tier architecture colleges like Sir J.J. College of Architecture (Mumbai) and CEPT University (Ahmedabad), a NATA score of 150+ out of 200 with 90%+ in 10+2 Boards (composite score 165+/200) is considered very safe. For RVCA and BMSCA Bengaluru, a NATA score between 135 and 150+ offers strong admission chances in Round 1 and Round 2.
What are the mandatory minimum qualifying marks in NATA 2026?
According to CoA guidelines, candidates must obtain at least 70 marks out of 200 overall to qualify. In addition, candidates must satisfy minimum sectional cutoffs in Part A (Drawing test - minimum ~20 marks out of 80) and Part B (Aptitude & Reasoning test - minimum ~30 marks out of 120).
Is there any negative marking in the NATA examination?
No, there is NO negative marking in any section of the NATA exam. Candidates receive marks for correct answers in Part B and subjective scores for artistic drawing proficiency in Part A without penalty for unattempted or incorrect questions.
Can I appear for multiple NATA sessions, and how does score selection work?
Yes, candidates are eligible to attempt up to three NATA tests in a single examination cycle. The Council of Architecture evaluates all attempts and issues a percentile scorecard reflecting the best overall score achieved across all valid attempts.
